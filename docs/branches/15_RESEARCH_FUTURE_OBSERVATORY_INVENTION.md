# Branch 15 — Research, Future Observatory & Invention

**Parent:** ATLAS Master Index & Genealogy  
**Lineage:** Human mission → ATLAS OS / Core → Research, Future Observatory & Invention  
**Status:** living branch dossier  
**Rule:** an idea enters this branch as a question or hypothesis—not as truth. Research may produce evidence, prototypes or invention candidates; only explicit validation gates may promote them into operational branches.

---

## 1. Branch purpose

Research & Future Observatory is the seed bank and experimental discipline of ATLAS. It protects creative freedom by giving new ideas a place to grow without forcing the rest of the ecosystem to treat them as finished products. Its fruit is better evidence, a reproducible result, a validated prototype, a defensible invention candidate or a decision to stop.

## 2. Roots inherited

- Questions are not conclusions.
- Hypotheses remain falsifiable and visibly uncertain.
- Negative results are preserved.
- Reproducibility and provenance outrank persuasive presentation.
- Safety, ethics and human impact are evaluated before promotion.
- Prototype success is not production proof.
- Novelty claims require prior-art review; patentability is never assumed.
- Confidential invention evidence is separated from public disclosure.
- Research involving humans/health/regulated domains receives domain-specific governance.
- A failed experiment is useful evidence when recorded honestly.

## 3. Trunk dependencies

Identity/Auth, Organization/DBA, Documents/Vault, Knowledge/Provenance, Audit Ledger, Projects, Reports/Analytics, Creator/Prototype assets, Global Context, domain branches, controlled sharing, security/IP controls and release verification.

## 4. Canonical sub-branches

### 4.1 Research Question Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Every research program should begin with a bounded question: problem, target population/system, context, known constraints, desired outcome, owner and source branch.

### 4.2 Hypothesis Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Hypotheses require statement, rationale, evidence for/against, assumptions, falsification condition, confidence/status and links to experiments/results.

### 4.3 Evidence / Source Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Research sources need type, citation/identifier, date, publisher/author, jurisdiction/population where relevant, quality/evidence level, conflicts and review status. Knowledge may present sources; Research owns the experimental/evidence-evaluation context.

### 4.4 Future Observatory — GREEN FRUIT

The established ATLAS Future Observatory evaluates concepts on dimensions including impact, safety, viability, novelty, reproducibility and evidence maturity.

**Fruit:** a transparent scorecard and recommendation—not a guarantee that the idea will work.

### 4.5 Novelty / Prior-Art Review — GREEN FRUIT

Invention candidates should be compared against patents, papers, products, standards and known public disclosures. Search results and review dates are preserved; absence of found prior art does not prove novelty.

### 4.6 Technical Feasibility — GREEN FRUIT

Feasibility analysis records required components, dependencies, performance targets, constraints, failure modes, costs and unknowns before large implementation investment.

### 4.7 Safety / Ethics Review — GREEN HIGH-GOVERNANCE

Potential harms, misuse, privacy, discrimination, physical/clinical/financial risk, vulnerable populations, environmental impact and mitigation plans are reviewed before high-risk experimentation or deployment.

### 4.8 Experiment / Protocol Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Experiments require hypothesis link, protocol version, materials/code/data, controls, metrics, acceptance/failure criteria, operator, dates and deviations.

### 4.9 Result / Observation Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Results preserve raw/processed evidence links, metrics, uncertainty, anomalies, operator notes and whether the stated acceptance criteria were met.

### 4.10 Reproducibility / Replication — GREEN FRUIT

A result becomes stronger when repeated independently or under documented conditions. Replication history includes environment/version differences and contradictory outcomes.

### 4.11 Negative / Null Results — GREEN FRUIT

ATLAS explicitly preserves experiments that fail, show no effect or contradict expectations. They remain searchable so future work does not unknowingly repeat the same dead end.

### 4.12 Prototype Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Prototype identity should record source idea/hypothesis, version, intended use, environment, known limitations, validation performed and files/code/assets. Prototype does not equal product.

### 4.13 Validation Gate — GREEN FRUIT

Promotion stages should be explicit, for example: seed → research → prototype → validated prototype → branch candidate → working fruit → ripe fruit. Each transition requires evidence appropriate to risk/intended use.

### 4.14 Invention Candidate Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Potential inventions record inventors/contributors, problem, mechanism, novelty rationale, prototype/evidence, disclosure dates, ownership/assignment status and IP handling decision.

### 4.15 Patent / Trade-Secret Decision — GREEN / PROFESSIONAL-BOUND

ATLAS can organize invention evidence and prior-art work. Patent filing, legal opinions, ownership/assignment and trade-secret policy require qualified legal/IP review where appropriate.

### 4.16 Confidential Disclosure / Lab Notebook — GREEN FRUIT

Research/invention records may require timestamps, authorship, hashes, witnesses/reviewers, controlled access and disclosure logs. Public publication must be separated from confidential evidence.

### 4.17 Health Frontiers — ARTIFACT-VERIFIED RESEARCH FRUIT

Health Frontiers artifacts demonstrate a research program framework with explicit rules against unsupported cure/treatment claims and with evidence/hypothesis separation. It belongs here for research governance while Health owns any future clinical intended use.

### 4.18 Digital Assets Regulatory Research — ARTIFACT-VERIFIED RESEARCH FRUIT

The Digital Assets regulatory-readiness dossier is a verified compliance/research artifact. It informs Commerce/Finance decisions but does not create legal authorization to issue, sell or custody assets.

### 4.19 AI / Machine-Learning Research — GREEN / SEED

Model research should record dataset/source rights, task/intended use, baseline, evaluation set, metrics, robustness/bias/safety tests, model/version and deployment boundary.

### 4.20 Model Evaluation / Benchmarking — GREEN FRUIT

Benchmarks must distinguish development from held-out evaluation, define metrics/thresholds, preserve dataset/version and report failure modes—not only aggregate scores.

### 4.21 Astronomy / Planetary Visualization — SEED / GREEN RESEARCH

ATLAS astronomy/planetary visualization is a research/education direction. Scientific values/textures/orbits must be sourced/versioned; visual fidelity is not scientific measurement accuracy by implication.

### 4.22 Spatial / Digital-Twin Research — GREEN CROSS-BRANCH

New mapping, simulation and holographic methods may be tested here before promotion into Robotics, Mobility, Health or Safety. Simulation assumptions and physical validation status remain visible.

### 4.23 New Materials / Manufacturing Research — SEED / GREEN

Robotics/Cars/hardware materials and manufacturing ideas need properties, source/vendor data, test method, safety/environmental constraints and manufacturability evidence.

### 4.24 Product / Business Experimentation — GREEN FRUIT

Pricing, workflow and market experiments require hypothesis, target group, consent/disclosure where needed, success metric and guardrails. Commercial experiments do not override consumer/legal requirements.

### 4.25 Research Data / Dataset Registry — GREEN FRUIT

Datasets require origin, license/consent, schema, population/coverage, collection period, transformations, quality, sensitive fields, permitted uses and retention.

### 4.26 Research Code / Environment Reproducibility — GREEN FRUIT

Code experiments should record repository/commit, dependencies, environment/container, seed/config, hardware and outputs so results can be rerun.

### 4.27 Research Collaboration / Partner Studies — PARTNER-BOUND

Universities, hospitals, companies, government or labs may collaborate under agreements defining data, IP, publication, confidentiality, ethics and responsibilities. Mentioning a prospective institution does not imply partnership.

### 4.28 Grants / Research Funding — GREEN / PARTNER-BOUND

ATLAS may organize grant opportunities, proposals, budgets, milestones and reports. Award/funding status must be provider/agency-confirmed; proposal submission is not award.

### 4.29 Regulatory / Standards Watch — GREEN FRUIT

High-change laws, standards and guidance should be tracked with source, jurisdiction, publication/effective date and impact assessment. Watch data informs branches but does not silently change production behavior without review/release.

### 4.30 Research Chronology / Decision Log — GREEN FRUIT

Material questions, decisions, evidence changes, failed approaches and promotions/pruning should be timestamped so future teams can reconstruct why ATLAS evolved.

### 4.31 Research-to-Branch Promotion — GREEN FRUIT

A promoted result must declare destination branch, authoritative system of record, new risks/permissions, migration plan, validation tests and which research assumptions remain unresolved.

### 4.32 Research Archive / Pruning — GREEN FRUIT

Abandoned ideas are archived rather than erased when their evidence could prevent repeated mistakes. Sensitive/private material follows retention/deletion obligations.

## 7. Fruit chains

`Problem → research question → evidence review → hypothesis → protocol → experiment → result → replication → decision`

`Idea → novelty/prior-art → feasibility/safety → prototype → validation → invention/IP decision → branch promotion or archive`

`Research artifact → domain review → intended-use validation → system-of-record design → implementation → release gate → working/ripe fruit`

## 8. Research invariants

1. Question is not conclusion.
2. Hypothesis is not fact.
3. Prototype is not production proof.
4. Negative/null results are preserved.
5. Every result retains protocol/data/code/version provenance.
6. Reproducibility status is explicit.
7. Safety/ethics review precedes high-risk promotion.
8. Novelty/patentability is never assumed from absence of an obvious competitor.
9. Research involving humans/health follows appropriate consent/ethics/regulatory governance.
10. AI benchmark scores disclose dataset/metric/version and failure modes.
11. Partner/institution names do not imply collaboration without agreement.
12. Proposal/grant submission does not imply award.
13. Confidential invention evidence is separated from public disclosure.
14. Promotion into a domain branch requires destination authority, validation and release evidence.

## 10. Definition of ripe fruit

A research output becomes **RIPE** only relative to a clearly stated claim and intended use after evidence quality, protocol, result integrity, reproducibility, safety/ethics, provenance and destination-domain validation are sufficient. Research can be successful even when the correct outcome is “do not build” or “evidence is insufficient.”
